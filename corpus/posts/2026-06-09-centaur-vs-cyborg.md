I thought I worked with AI like a centaur. Turns out I'm a cyborg.

A centaur keeps a clean line between human and machine. You do what you're best at, the AI does what it's best at, you combine the outputs. A clear, methodical division of labour.

A cyborg intertwines. You start something, hand it to the AI, take it back, reshape it, hand it again. The line between your thinking and the AI's output blurs. You're not sure anymore where one ends and the other begins.

Ethan Mollick argues both modes are valid. But they require different disciplines. The centaur needs to know clearly where its edge is. The cyborg needs to keep checking that it's still steering — that the intertwining hasn't quietly handed over the wheel.

The back-and-forth is how I find the final shape of something. But I've learned to ask myself periodically: am I still directing this, or am I just approving?

Centaur or cyborg — are you sure you're still the one steering?
