I had three nights where I couldn't sleep.

Not from worry. From possibility.

I'd been using AI as a tool for months — prompting, getting answers, moving on. Then something shifted. I started seeing not just what it could do today, but what it implied. The combinations. The things that weren't possible six months ago. I stayed up not because I had to, but because I didn't want to stop thinking.

Ethan Mollick describes this moment in Co-Intelligence — the crossing of a threshold where skepticism breaks and something else floods in. He frames it as a civilisation-scale event. I can only describe my version of it: the moment I stopped being a user and started building things with it — small workflows, custom prompts, a system that did work I couldn't have done alone.

I don't think that shift happens the same way twice, and I don't think it happens from reading about AI. It happens from using it until something surprises you. The sleepless nights aren't a warning sign. They're the signal that you've arrived somewhere real.

What was your version of that moment?
