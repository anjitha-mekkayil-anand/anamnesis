One sentence stopped me mid-chapter.

"Humans (walking and talking bags of water and trace chemicals that we are) have managed to convince well-organized sand to pretend to think like us."

— Ethan Mollick, *Co-Intelligence*

What gets me is the parenthetical. "Walking and talking bags of water and trace chemicals that we are." The wit is in there — the self-deprecation, the reminder that we are also, at bottom, just physics. We didn't create intelligence from nothing — we coaxed a reflection of it from silicon.

And "pretend to think." Not "think." Mollick doesn't resolve the question. It reads to me as deliberately unresolved.

Most explanations try to demystify it — here's how the transformer works, here's what RLHF does. This sentence does the opposite. It makes the mystery more precise.

The more I work with these tools, the more I'm surprised by what we are.

That we could pour ourselves into text across centuries — every book, every argument, every pattern of thought — and that the sand learned to pretend.

What's a sentence about AI that stopped you mid-chapter?
