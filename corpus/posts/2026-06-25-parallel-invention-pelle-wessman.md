A developer I've never met in Sweden built the same thing I built. He called it vp-knowledge. I called it Noesis.

The idea came from Andrej Karpathy — that your notes should work like a second brain. Queryable. Connected. Maintained over time, not abandoned in a folder somewhere.

I took that and added an LLM: not just storing notes, but having the model maintain the connections between them. Every session, new things get woven into what's already there.

Pelle Wessman presented at a Claude Code meetup in Malmö in May. His system: local markdown files, an LLM maintaining them between sessions, wikilinks as edges. 1,100 interconnected notes built over years. His key line: "Every session has insights. Most of them I lose." That's exactly the problem.

I found out after I'd already built mine.

Parallel invention is one of the stranger experiences in engineering — you solve a problem because it seems real and the solution seems obvious, and then you find out someone arrived at the same answer independently, from a different direction, at roughly the same time. Something confirming in that. The problem was real enough to solve twice.

If two people build the same thing without knowing about each other, they were both right about the problem.

What have you built that turned out to have a parallel?
