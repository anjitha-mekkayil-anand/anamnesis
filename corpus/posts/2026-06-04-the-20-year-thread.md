This week I started building a new project - an AI agent that watches industrial sensor data and reasons about what it's seeing. Not "this reading is too high," but "vibration is climbing while temperature follows - that's early bearing wear, here's the evidence."

Then I went digging through some old files, and found the first real program I ever wrote.

2006, final year of engineering. A factory monitoring system: it read temperature and level sensors in a plant, watched for anything drifting out of range, and acted on it without a person standing over it. Written in assembly, on a microcontroller. I was 21.

Then I found a paper I published in 2015 - a real-time sensor monitoring system on a single-board computer. It logged readings, plotted them live, and pushed an alert to my phone when something crossed a threshold.

Same instinct, three times, across twenty years. Watch the signal, notice what doesn't belong, say something before it breaks.

I had started building the new one before I remembered I'd already built it twice.

What changed wasn't me. It was what I had to work with. In 2006 the decision was a hardcoded comparison. In 2015, a threshold. In 2026, it can finally reason about the pattern itself.

The AI didn't hand me a new direction. It caught up to an old one.

I think a lot of us have a thread like this - something we kept reaching for long before the tools were good enough to meet us there.

What's the thread you've been pulling on longer than you realised?
