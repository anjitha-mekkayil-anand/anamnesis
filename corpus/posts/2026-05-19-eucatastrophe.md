Tolkien coined a word: eucatastrophe. The opposite of catastrophe — a sudden turn toward grace. The unearned good outcome. The story pivots not because it was fought for, but because something larger turned.

Early on, I spent months moving carefully around systems I didn't understand. The knowledge wasn't written down — it lived in the heads of people who'd been there longest. You learned by sitting next to the right person, or making the expensive mistake.

Now I'm sometimes that person. And the same gap forms with every new joiner. It isn't ability. It's access to years of accumulated context.

What interests me about AI is specific. Not replacing senior intuition — most of it isn't written down and never will be — but making the written residue legible. The commit message that explains why. The half-deleted PR thread. The comment from someone who left three years ago.

Someone at 11pm, asking "why does this work this way?" might get an answer now. Not the same as sitting next to the right person. But not nothing.

Tolkien's eucatastrophe was a grace. It arrived. The version of me at 11pm a long time ago might have had somewhere to ask.
